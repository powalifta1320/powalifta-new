## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** The steel-headline + chalk-dust + red force-line is a distinctive, committed signature that lands the briefed "Kinetic Steel + Chalk Force" direction. It dodges every saturated reflex (no SaaS-cream, no editorial-serif drop-caps, no glassy crypto-gradient). The aesthetic lane — Linear/Vercel structural rigor × fight-poster heat — is held.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` on `index.html` → **0 findings (clean)**.

**The one honest tension:** the steel headline is `background-clip:text` + gradient — which is on the skill's absolute-ban list ("gradient text"). Here it's a *deliberate, briefed brand moment*, not lazy decoration, so identity-preservation wins — **but only if it stays legible.** Measured: the 51% gradient stop `#4f5663` renders at **2.66:1** against `#0b0b0c`, below the 3:1 large-text AA floor. That's the line between "earns its gradient" and "fails the ban." Fixable in one stop.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely strong hero — the kind that makes someone stop. The steel type reads as forged metal, the red force-line gives it a pulse, and the three-CTA triage is sharp IA. The biggest opportunity isn't adding more; it's making sure the signature survives the edges: reduced-motion, mobile, and the darkest band of the gradient.

## What's Working

1. **The steel headline is a real signature moment.** Vertical brushed-metal gradient + travelling rim-light sweep + CSS-first fallback (the `<h1>` is never gated on JS, so a headless render or failed script still ships readable type). Distinctive and bulletproof — rare combination.
2. **Color discipline.** One red (`#ff2d3f`, 5.34:1) used as a weapon — force-line, "BARBELL.", primary CTA — never as garnish. This is the "committed" brand strategy done right.
3. **Audience triage in the hero.** Solo / coached / coach as three CTAs maps the three user types without diluting the headline. Good information architecture.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Steel headline dips below AA contrast at its darkest band.** The `#4f5663` mid-stop = 2.66:1 vs the near-black bg (large-text AA needs 3:1). Perceptually fine at 147px, but it's a real AA miss and it's the exact thing that justifies keeping the gradient-text. **Fix:** raise the 51% stop from `#4f5663` to ~`#646b78` (≈3.2:1) — keeps the brushed-steel dip, clears the floor. Command: `/impeccable audit` (or `typeset`).
- **[P2] The signature is desktop-fine-pointer-only.** The red force-line + chalk billow live entirely on a JS canvas gated to `innerWidth ≥ 760` + fine pointer + motion-allowed. Mobile and reduced-motion users get the steel headline but *none* of the "red energy thread" the brand leans on. **Fix:** add a static CSS red thread/underline behind the headline for the no-canvas path so the signature isn't a desktop privilege. Command: `/impeccable adapt`.
- **[P2] Atmospheric layer-stacking in the hero.** Steel sheen + chalk dust + force-line + "POWALIFTA" ghost watermark + grid overlay all occupy the same fold. The grid is dimmed to 0.4 (good instinct), but on a brighter display the watermark risks fighting the headline. **Fix:** pull the ghost watermark opacity/scale down a touch, or let it fade faster on scroll so the headline owns the peak moment. Command: `/impeccable quieter` (hero only).
- **[P3] Headline scale (147px) exceeds the 96px general ceiling.** This is deliberate fight-poster shouting and on-brand — flagging only so it's a conscious choice. Responsive clamp verified clean (88px @768, 45px @375, no overflow, no horizontal scroll). No action needed unless you want to test it against the longest possible headline copy.
- **[P3] Eyebrow-grammar risk below the fold.** The single hero kicker "POWERLIFTING'S NEW HOME" is fine as voice. But if the same uppercase-tracked eyebrow pattern repeats above *every* section down the page, that flips from voice to AI scaffolding. **Fix:** vary the section-intro cadence so the kicker isn't a reflex. Command: `/impeccable typeset` (full page).

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer):** "e1RM" and "RPE-based logging" in the sub-headline assume powerlifting fluency. A lifter who tracks in a notebook may not know "e1RM" means estimated 1-rep-max. The demo rescues this, but the sub-copy could decode one term inline.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile):** Gets the steel headline but loses the entire motion signature (canvas is desktop-gated) — acceptable, but the red "energy thread" that defines the brand is absent on the device most first-touches happen on. Three CTAs stack fine; tap targets need a quick 44px check on the smallest breakpoint.

**Riley (Stress Tester):** Headless/reduced-motion render correctly falls back to static steel (verified — the `<h1>` isn't gated on JS). Force-line + chalk simply don't appear; nothing breaks. Good defensive design. The only edge to probe: extremely long headline copy against the 147px scale on a 1024–1280 tablet.
